The Walking Water Field Trip

Two cups of colored water, one empty cup, and a folded paper towel. Wait a while and the colors climb the towel, cross over, and mix in the empty cup. Run it here, shrink down to see why it works, then try it with real cups.

Stop 2 · Real World

Do It With Real Cups

Get your supplies together and check them off. Your checklist is saved in this browser.

Supplies

Heads up: Food coloring stains clothes and counters. Set up on a tray and wear an old shirt. Don't drink the water when you're done.

Steps

  1. Line up 7 clear cups in a row (a circle works too and makes a color wheel).
  2. Fill cups 1, 3, 5 and 7 about ¾ full of water. Leave cups 2, 4 and 6 empty.
  3. Add about 10 drops of coloring: red in 1 and 7, yellow in 3, blue in 5. Stir.
  4. Fold each paper towel lengthwise in half, then in half again, to make a long skinny strip. Trim to about 2 cups tall.
  5. Bend each strip into an arch, with one end in a full cup and the other in the empty cup next to it.
  6. Start the Field Trip Timer and check every 30 minutes. Let it sit overnight to see the finish.

      Stop 5 · Side Trips

      More Experiments Like This One

      Each one uses the same ideas as walking water: capillary action, dissolving, or color mixing.

      Rainbow Celery

      Plants drink through tiny tubes called xylem. Colored water shows you the path.

      How to do it
      1. Put 20 drops of coloring in a cup of water.
      2. Have an adult trim the bottom of a celery stalk (keep the leaves on).
      3. Stand it in the cup and wait 1–2 days.
      4. Slice the stalk across and look for colored dots. Those are the xylem tubes.

      Marker Chromatography

      Black marker ink is made of several dyes. Water carries each one a different distance.

      How to do it
      1. Draw a thick dot of washable black marker in the middle of a coffee filter.
      2. Poke a small hole in the dot and push a rolled paper-towel "wick" through.
      3. Set the filter on a cup of water so only the wick touches the water.
      4. Watch rings of purple, blue and pink spread out.

      Candy Rainbow Plate

      The colored candy coating dissolves and spreads through warm water in stripes.

      How to do it
      1. Put a ring of colorful coated candies around the edge of a white plate.
      2. Gently pour warm water into the middle until it touches every candy.
      3. Don't bump the plate! Watch the stripes spread toward the center.
      4. Try cold water next time. Which is faster?

      Penny Drop Challenge

      Water molecules hold onto each other (cohesion) so tightly they make a dome.

      How to do it
      1. Lay a dry penny flat.
      2. Guess how many drops of water fit on it.
      3. Add drops one at a time with an eyedropper or straw, counting each one.
      4. Watch the dome grow until it spills. Was your guess close? (20–40 is common!)

      Towel Race

      Which paper wicks fastest? The size of the gaps between fibers changes the speed.

      How to do it
      1. Cut equal strips of paper towel, napkin, toilet paper and coffee filter.
      2. Dip all the ends into colored water at the same moment.

      Walking Water Color Wheel

      Put the cups in a circle (red, empty, yellow, empty, blue, empty) and you get a full color wheel.

      How to do it
      1. Arrange 6 cups in a ring.
      2. Fill every other cup and color them red, yellow and blue.
      3. Bridge every neighbor pair, including the last cup back to the first.
      4. You'll get orange, green and purple between the primaries.
